00.5 · What The Research Suggests

One pattern across six years of public record.

Across the Series A coverage, founder interviews, the Instagram presence, and the singular Egmore flagship, the same picture appears to emerge. CHOSEN reads as a clinical skincare brand of national consequence, while operating largely as a Chennai-based DTC.

The publicly cited Rx network counts over two thousand prescribing clinics, with one branded retail space. The combined founder + brand social audience approaches three hundred and twenty thousand followers, with no formal activations at the hotels, members clubs, or IT-corridor wellness programmes the founder has named publicly as B2B priorities.

Three gaps appear consistently. All look addressable within ninety days.

The Rx network may be a retail engine in waiting.

Roughly two thousand dermatologists prescribe CHOSEN today. Their patients typically leave a consultation problem-aware, with a brand and a product in mind. Few of those patients currently walk past a CHOSEN counter on the way out of the clinic.

A small branded shelf inside fifty high-prescribing clinics across five metros could compound the Rx loop into recurring shelf revenue, at an estimated CapEx of ₹35–60K per corner. This is the lowest-CapEx, highest-fit channel in the playbook — and the recommended lead move.

07 · The Tier-3 Pilot Pitch
07 · The Tier-3 Pilot Pitch

Park Hyatt Chennai. F&B Director.

The hotel pilot sits at tier 3 of the channel ladder, recommended only once two clinic corners are operational. The Park Hyatt is proposed because younger F&B clientele and a more agile contracting process should reduce the legal cycle. The pitch below is an example, not a sent letter.

Subject ·  A four-week wellness installation. No rent expected.
Opening
Dr. Renita Rajan's CHOSEN is one of the few Indian skincare brands with peer-reviewed clinical publications and India's first Melanin Lab. The brand has not yet partnered with a hotel in Chennai. We believe the Park Hyatt would be the right first one.
Our proposal
A four-week installation in the spa lounge — a curated three-SKU display (SAFESCREEN, Re(d)covery, TOR Collagen), an in-room ₹150 mini-SPF amenity for sixty rooms across fourteen days, and one Saturday-evening masterclass — "Skin Drinks & Sun Protection" — hosted by Dr. Rajan in person.
Our ask
Use of eight square feet in the spa lounge. Two house-list emails to spa members. Permission for the in-room amenity.
Our give
15% revenue share on all in-house sales during the window. Complimentary CHOSEN gift kits for the spa team (~₹40,000 retail value). Subject to founder consent, a co-branded reel from Dr. Rajan's personal account. For the Park Hyatt brand, this could read as wellness positioning at low acquisition cost.

13 · What Could Go Wrong
13 · What Could Go Wrong

Four implementation risks. Mitigations stated.

A plan without a risk register is a wish-list. The four points below are the most likely places this playbook could break, with the contingencies recommended at the start of execution rather than after.

R-01 · Partner reluctance
Medium
Clinics may resist a branded counter.
Some dermatologists could perceive a CHOSEN shelf as endorsing one brand over the broader prescription set, especially in clinics that prescribe a mix of brands.
Mitigation
Lead with the 10 highest-prescribing CHOSEN clinics — relationships are already warm. Offer revenue share with complimentary inventory rotation rather than a flat placement fee. Keep all arrangements non-exclusive so the clinic can still recommend other brands when clinically appropriate.
R-02 · Founder bandwidth
High
The model leans heavily on Dr. Rajan's in-person time.
The Park Hyatt masterclass, the Bangalore reactivation evening and the Pune Conrad visit each suggest a founder appearance — three in-person events across two cities within ninety days is operationally demanding.
Mitigation
Cap founder visits at one per month. Train a senior partner-dermatologist to lead all non-founder activations, opened by a pre-recorded Dr. Rajan video introduction so the brand voice remains consistent.
R-03 · Hotel-channel conversion
Medium
Hotel CPQC could run higher than projected.
The worked example projects ~₹659 CPQC at the Park Hyatt. If spa guests remain in leisure mode rather than problem-aware mode, CPQC could land closer to the Drop threshold, and one or more hotel partnerships might close mid-Phase II.
Mitigation
Pre-commit kill criteria in writing with the partner. Stage hotel activations only after the clinic channel has shown ROI ≥ 2.0×. Cap hotel CapEx at ₹2L per pilot. Prepare the Cycle-2 corrective format (weekday-morning consult, co-hosted with a derm partner) before Cycle 1 launches.
R-04 · Inventory & fulfilment
Low
A stock-out at a corner can damage brand more than the activation lifts it.
Running out of SAFESCREEN minis at the Park Hyatt — or hero SKUs at a clinic corner — during a 4-week run risks turning the activation into a negative brand event.
Mitigation
Weekly inventory cron from every corner into a single Slack channel. A 1.5× buffer stocked at the Egmore flagship for 24-hour emergency replenishment. Daily-stand-up review of any corner running below 30% of par stock.
R-05 · Compliance & ethics
Low likelihood · High impact
Regulatory line on POS adjacent to prescription.
CDSCO rules on promotional claims, the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, and dermatologist prescription ethics shape how clinic corners can be staffed and signed. A POS that looks like a kickback adjacent to a prescription pad creates real exposure for the clinic partner — and reputational exposure for the brand.
Mitigation
Legal review of all POS collateral before Cycle 1. An explicit "no financial inducement to prescriber" clause in every clinic MoU. Clinic-corner staffing by CHOSEN brand ambassadors, never by clinic personnel. Quarterly compliance audit by Phase III.
